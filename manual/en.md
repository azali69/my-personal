My Personal Quran is a Madinah mushaf for reading, memorising (hafazan), listening and daily worship. This manual explains every screen and button, in the order you meet them.

> **Disclaimer.** My Personal Quran is for **personal use only**. It is free, not for sale, and must not be used commercially. Its contents belong to their publishers and are shown unchanged with credit: the Qur'an text from the King Fahd Complex (KFGQPC); translations and tafsir from QuranEnc.com; word by word and transliteration from Quran.com; hadith texts from the Hadith API (fawazahmed0) and Sunnah.com, with the gradings of the scholars named on each hadith; explanations from HadeethEnc.com; Hisnul Muslim from hisnmuslim.com; prayer times, khutbah and halal certificates from MUIS; and the Sirah, Sifat Solat and Solat Jumaat written for this app from named classical sources (Settings → Sources & verification lists them all). The app is a study aid, not a fatwa: for rulings, ask a qualified scholar.

## 1. Getting started

**Install (Android)**

1. Download the latest **My-Personal-Quran-v1.0.0.<build>.apk** (for example My-Personal-Quran-v1.0.0.65.apk) you were sent.
2. Open it on the phone. The first time, Android asks you to allow installs from that app (e.g. Files or WhatsApp); allow it.
3. Tap **Install**. A newer APK installs over the old one and keeps your bookmarks, blocks, Hafazan history and settings.

**First launch**

- The **book cover** appears. Tap anywhere on it to open the book; it swings open to the **Home** screen.
- The cover comes back when you have been out of the app for more than 3 minutes (a quick trip to Google Translate or a source link does not bring it back). **Tap the top-left corner** of the cover to turn it off (turn it on again in Settings › Home); **tap the top-right corner** to change its colour; by default Home and the top and bottom bars change with it (Home settings → **"Tap change colour" on the book cover changes** → **Book cover only** to change just the cover). To skip it in settings: Home → settings button (top right) → **Book cover when the app opens → Skip**.
- The cover has a gold frame, corner flowers and the medallion with القرآن الكريم, and "My Personal Quran" below. Its colour: same settings button → **Book cover colour**: **Same as home** (follows the Home background), **Madinah green**, **Black**, **Night blue**, **Light blue**, **Pastel green** or **Ivory**. **Preview the cover** shows it straight away.
- Opening the app from a prayer notification skips the cover and goes straight to **Prayer times**.

**Permissions** (each asked only when you first use the feature)

| Permission | Asked when | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Microphone | First Hafazan, Listen & repeat or voice search | To hear and record your recitation |
| Notifications | You turn on a prayer alert | To show prayer alerts and reminders |
| Alarms & reminders | Prayer alerts | So alerts come on the minute |
| Location | Prayer times "My location", or Qibla | To calculate times and the Qibla direction |

The Qur'an text, translations and Hafazan work offline. Audio, tafsir, hadith, word-by-word meanings and Hisnul Muslim need the internet the first time; most are then kept on the phone.

**Gestures on the page**

- **Swipe** left or right: turn the page; it curls like paper.
- Settings → **Turning pages** → **Scroll (continuous)**: the pages follow one another as you scroll down instead (swiping then does nothing; ‹ › still jump a page).
- **Tap** the page: hide or show the bars.
- **Long-press an ayah** and let go: the **ayah menu**.
- **Long-press a word, keep holding and drag** to the last word: select words. A bar appears with **My ruku'**, **My blocks**, **Erase**, **Hafazan**, **Listen** and **Copy**.
- **Tap once** anywhere on the page: show or hide the bars. With Settings → **Tap a word twice: meaning and sound → On**, **tap a word twice** quickly to see its meaning and hear it (section 7); tap twice again, or tap outside, to close it.

**The ayah menu** (long-press an ayah)

- **Bookmark** in one of six colours, or remove it.
- **Listen from this ayah** · **Listen & repeat** · **Hafazan from this ayah to the end of the page** · **Hafazan this block**.
- **Add My ruku' or My block from here**.
- **Word by word** · **Tafsir** · **Hadith** · **Copy ayat and translation**.

**Page styles** (Settings → Mushaf)

- **Madinah 1441H**: the printed King Fahd Complex page, 15 lines. Settings → **Save all 604 pages** keeps them for offline reading.
- **Madinah tajweed**: the same page with tajweed colours. Settings → Mushaf → **Colour key** explains them: dark red Madd Lazim (6), orange-red Madd Wajib Muttasil (4-5), light orange Madd 'Arid li as-Sukun (2/4/6), yellow Madd Tabi'i (2), green ghunnah, light blue qalqalah, dark blue tafkhim, grey silent letters. Izhar, idgham and ikhfa' are not coloured. (Source: Tarteel's guide to these fonts.)
- **Uthmani text**: text in the King Fahd font; **Fit page** (15 lines on screen) or **Larger text** (scroll).

**Translation view**: each ayah with Bahasa Melayu (Abdullah Basmeih) or English (Saheeh International), optional English footnotes and transliteration. Transliteration is approximate and always shown under the Arabic.

**Search**: type Arabic, Malay or English words, a reference like 2:255, or a page number. The microphone searches by voice; **Auto** detects the language.

## 4. Blocks: waqaf & ibtida', My ruku', My blocks

Blocks shade the page in sections so you can memorise and revise one at a time. Choose with the **two stacked bars** button or Settings → **Waqaf & ibtida' blocks**:

| Mode | Shading |
| --- | --- |
| Off | None (waqf signs always show) |
| Ruku' (original) | The original ruku' sections (ع), one section shaded light gold, the next unshaded, in turn through the whole Qur'an |
| My ruku' | Blue, your own ruku' sections |
| My blocks | Your own blocks, any length |

**Make a block**

1. Long-press the first word, keep holding, drag to the last word.
2. Let go and tap **My ruku'** or **My blocks** in the bar.
3. Or use the two-bars button and enter the start and end by numbers.

**Erase**: select words the same way and tap **Erase**. **Use a block**: long-press an ayah inside it → **Hafazan this block**.

## 5. Hafazan (recite from memory)

The text is hidden; each word appears as you recite it correctly.

1. Tap **Hafazan** (bottom bar or Home).
2. Choose **This page**, **the block you are on**, a **Custom range**, or **Carry on from where you stopped**.
3. **Stop at**: **End of the range**, or **Until I stop** (keeps going into the next surahs).
4. **Record my voice**: Record or Do not record.
5. Tap **Start**, then the round **microphone** at the bottom right, and recite.

While reciting: the page scrolls with you; tap the page to hide the bars; **Show text** reveals the words faintly; **Type** lets you type instead.

Tap the microphone again to stop and see the result:

| Colour | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Normal text | Correct |
| Red (tap to see what was heard) | Wrong word |
| Orange, wavy underline | Skipped |
| Dotted underline | Unclear |

- Press the microphone again to **carry on** in the same session; recited words stay marked.
- **New session from x:y** starts fresh where you stopped. **Start again** clears the marks.
- Settings → **Hafazan history** keeps past sessions, scores and recordings (newest 30).
- **Microphone settings** (Android 13+): quiet-voice boost, noisy/windy mode, Bluetooth headset.

The app hears words, not tajwid, and can mishear; check flagged words against the text.

## 6. Listening to reciters

Tap the **headphones** or long-press an ayah → **Listen from this ayah**.

| Reciter | How it plays |
| --- | --- |
| Mishary Rashid Alafasy | Word by word in red |
| Abdur-Rahman as-Sudais | Word by word in red |
| Yasser ad-Dussary | Word by word (follows his repeats) |
| Maher al-Muaiqly | Ayah by ayah |
| Maher al-Muaiqly (whole surah) | One recording per surah |
| Hazza al-Balushi | Whole surahs (91 of 114) |

- **Repeat each ayah**: 1× to 10×. **When the range ends**: Stop, Play again, or Carry on to the next surahs.
- **Red words follow the voice**: a later setting if the red runs ahead, an earlier one if it lags.
- **Speed**: 0.75×, 1×, 1.25×.
- The small player bar: play/pause, previous, next, stop. Headset and Bluetooth buttons work too. When the bars are hidden while it plays, the **page number** at the bottom shows ▶ / ❚❚: tap it to pause or play; tap the page anywhere else to bring the bars back.
- On the phone the recitation keeps playing with the screen off or in another app, and **Carry on to the next surah** goes on by itself; a **Recitation playing** notification (with Pause, Next, Stop) shows while it plays. For whole-surah reciters with missing surahs (Hazza al-Balushi), it goes on to the next surah that is available.

**Listen & repeat**

1. The reciter recites one part; the microphone then opens by itself (or waits, with **Wait for me**).
2. Recite it, then press the red **Stop**.
3. Words turn green (correct), red (wrong) or orange (skipped). **▶ Reciter** and **▶ Me** compare both recordings.
4. Recite again, or **Next ›** / **‹ Back**.

**Each part is**: **Waqf to waqf** (Alafasy, as-Sudais or Yasser) or **One ayah** (any reciter). The check hears words only; judge tajwid by ear and with a teacher.

## 7. Word by word, tafsir, hadith, copy

All open from the **ayah menu**; they need the internet once per surah.

- **Word by word**: each word's meaning (Indonesian or English); tap a word to see it, then **Play word** to hear it (or choose **Play automatically**). Settings → **Tap a word twice: meaning and sound** opens it by tapping a word twice on the page.
- **Tafsir**: Al-Mukhtasar (Indonesian or English) or As-Sa'di (Indonesian); **Show Arabic original** adds the Arabic. From QuranEnc.com, unchanged.
- **Hadith**: the hadith linked by the books themselves (Kitab at-Tafsir of Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim), with the chapter heading, number, Arabic, translation and a link to Sunnah.com; then **In the eight hadith books in this app**: every hadith whose Arabic quotes the ayah word for word (or names it, e.g. "2.255"), tap one to read it with its grading. Ayat that have hadith show a small **book mark** on the ayah number in the mushaf (about 900 ayat).
- **Copy**: choose the ayat and what to include, then **Copy**.
- **Ayat with similar wording**: other ayat that share at least 5 words in a row with this ayah (the similar wording huffaz compare when memorising), longest first. Tap one to go to it. Found by matching the Arabic words; common set phrases alone are not counted. It is not a tafsir cross-reference.

## 8. Doa & zikir, prayer times, Qibla

**Doa & zikir (Hisnul Muslim)**

1. Home → **Doa & zikir**. **Morning zikir**, **Evening zikir**, before sleeping and on waking are at the top under **Daily**; your saved chapters are under **Saved**; then **All chapters**, grouped by topic (e.g. **Prayer (solat)** holds every part of the prayer, **After prayer** the zikir after it). Tap a topic to open or close it. Search finds a chapter by topic.
2. **Morning zikir** and **Evening zikir** are the book's chapter of morning and evening remembrance as two sets: what the book gives for the morning only is only in the morning set, what it gives for the evening only is only in the evening set, and the evening set uses the evening wording (أمسينا، أمسيت…) where the book gives one. Entries with the evening wording have no Listen button, as the recording is of the morning wording.
3. Each doa shows Arabic, transliteration and English. **Listen** plays it; the **0 / 3** counter counts repetitions.
4. **Source** under each doa: tap it for the reference given in the book (e.g. "Al-Bukhari …; Muslim …") with a link to that entry on sunnah.com. Its first line sums up the grading from that reference: cited from Sahih al-Bukhari / Muslim; graded sahih or hasan by the scholars named; authentic as the words of a Companion; or no grading given in the book.
5. The title and the on/off buttons stay at the top while you scroll. Each chapter has **Bookmark** (five colours, as in Hadith), **Note** and **Copy**. **Continue reading** and your **Bookmarks** (tick **Show**; filter by colour) are at the top of Doa & zikir, the same layout as Hadith. Chapters saved in the older version became gold bookmarks.

**Hadith (Kutub as-Sittah and al-Muwatta)**

1. Home → **Hadith**. Tick what to show, **Arabic**, **Bahasa Indonesia** and/or **English** (at least one; the same boxes are at the top of each kitab), then a book: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Sunan an-Nasa'i, Sunan Ibn Majah or Muwatta Malik.
2. Open a kitab to read its hadith: Arabic with harakat, then the translation. More hadith load as you scroll.
3. **Search all eight books** (at the top of the Hadith page) looks through every book at once, in Arabic, English or Indonesian; the first time it downloads each book (about 80 MB in all, Wi-Fi recommended). **Hadith number** + **Go** jumps to a hadith. **Search** looks through the whole book in Arabic, English or Indonesian (the first search downloads the book).
4. Gradings appear above each hadith of the four Sunan: al-Albani, Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut, Zubair 'Ali Za'i, and for at-Tirmidhi his own verdict in Arabic. Green is sahih, blue hasan, orange da'if, red very weak or fabricated. Bukhari and Muslim are sahih by the agreement of the scholars, so they have no per-hadith grade. The Muwatta has one grading, Salim al-Hilali's; Mawquf means the words of a Companion and Maqtu' the words of a Successor, not of the Prophet ﷺ. The Muwatta is always shown in full (with its chain).
5. The copy button copies the Arabic, the translation, the reference and the gradings.
6. Each kitab needs the internet the first time; after that it is kept on the phone.
7. Hadith show the **text only** at first, with "Narrated by …" (the Companion) above it. Where the Companion only says "I heard" or "that" before the Prophet ﷺ, the text starts at "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ" / "the Prophet ﷺ". Tick **SANAD** to see the full chain of narrators; the chain is then shown in a softer colour so the text stands out. Where the chain could not be separated with certainty, the hadith is shown in full.
8. On a book's page, its title, **Hadith number** and **Search this book** stay at the top too. The kitab title and the **AR · BI · ENG · SANAD** boxes stay at the top while you scroll; tap once on the text to hide them, and again to show them (the same in Doa & zikir and the Sirah). Tap the title to jump to another kitab of the same book.
9. Hadith that HadeethEnc.com explains have a **Syarah · HadeethEnc** button under the text. It opens their explanation, lessons from the hadith and word meanings, in **BM** (Bahasa Melayu, for about 100 hadith so far), **BI**, **ENG** or **AR**, unchanged from HadeethEnc. It is linked to the hadith by matching the text, so it may quote another narration of the same hadith.
10. **Also in** under a hadith lists the same hadith in the other books (e.g. "Muslim 113", "Abu Dawud 3692"). "(close wording)" means most, not all, of the wording matches, or only part of a longer hadith. Tap one to read it with that book's own grading, then **Open in …** to go there. They are found by comparing the Arabic text, so they help you find other narrations; they do not mean the chains or gradings are the same.
11. Each hadith has a **bookmark** button (five colours, as for the Qur'an) and a **note** button. **Continue reading** (where you last stopped) is at the top of the Hadith page. Your bookmarks are listed below it when **Show** (beside **All**) is ticked; it is off at first.

12. Each book has its own **Continue reading** at the top of its page (also Syama'il). The book's introduction folds into **About this book**; tap it to read. Under each hadith: **Maktabah Syamilah ›** (the opening words are copied and a step-by-step guide opens; see *Searching in Maktabah Syamilah* below) and **Scholars' gradings & syarah · Dorar.net ›** (Dorar shows the verdicts of the muhaddithin, e.g. al-Albani, Ibn Hajar, adh-Dhahabi, al-Haythami, with the reason a hadith is weak, and links to its syarah). Hadith gradings show each grader's death year.

**Searching in Maktabah Syamilah** (shamela.ws, for syarah, takhrij and other narrations):

1. Under the hadith, tap **Maktabah Syamilah ›**. The opening words of the matn (without harakat) are copied and the guide opens.
2. Tap **Open Maktabah Syamilah**. The search page opens at بحث في محتوى الكتب (search the contents of the books).
3. Long-press the search box → **Paste**. Quotation marks "..." search the exact wording; without them any of the words match.
4. Optional: بحث في نطاق محدد, then الكتب (one book, e.g. صحيح البخاري) or الأقسام (a section).
5. Tap بحــث (Search).
6. In نتائج البحث, filter with تصفية النتائج بالأقسام: شروح الحديث (commentaries such as Fath al-Bari, an-Nawawi's Sharh Muslim), التخريج والأطراف (takhrij and gradings), العلل والسؤلات الحديثية ('ilal), كتب السنة (other hadith books).
7. Tap a result to open that page; note the book, volume and page as your reference.
8. No results? Search 3 to 5 less common words without quotation marks; narrations differ in wording.
13. **Asy-Syama'il al-Muhammadiyyah** (at-Tirmidhi) has its own **Syama'il** tile on Home, and is also the eighth book in the Hadith list: the Prophet's appearance, manners and daily life ﷺ, 400 hadith in 56 chapters, in Arabic and English (Sunnah.com). Each hadith shows the grading of **Zubair 'Ali Za'i** and, where the hadith is in al-Albani's *Mukhtasar asy-Syama'il*, **al-Albani**'s grading with his number. He left out repeated chains, so a missing grading of his does not mean weak. **Also in** shows the same hadith in the six books, with their own gradings (al-Albani, Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut, Zubair 'Ali Za'i). There is no open Malay translation of this book, so BI shows the English.

**Sifat Solat Nabi ﷺ**

1. Home → **Sifat Solat Nabi ﷺ**: the prayer in 17 steps, from the intention to the salam, then **dhikr and du'a after the prayer**, and qunut in Subuh.
2. Each step has a short description and its **hadith** from the seven books with their gradings; tap one to read it (the phone's back button returns to the step).
3. **Where scholars differ** (marked "scholars differ" in the list) sets the four schools, **Shafi'i**, **Hanafi**, **Maliki** and **Hanbali**, side by side; below them, **Other scholars' views** opens a separate box with **Imam al-Nawawi**, **Ibn Taymiyyah**, **al-Shatibi**, **al-Albani**, **Ibn Baz**, **Ibn 'Uthaymin** and **Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut** (his gradings of reports outside this app's books, e.g. Hulb at-Ta'i's 'on the chest') **Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah** (Egypt's fatwa house) and the **Perlis State Fatwa Committee** where they spoke on the point, each marked \*, with its **reference** (book and volume/page) and **Open source ›** (the scholar's fatwa site, Dorar.net's fiqh encyclopedia or IslamQA). Only views with a reference are included. The evidence discussed is listed with its grading, without choosing between them: e.g. where to place the hands, the basmalah aloud, al-Fatihah behind the imam, knees or hands first, the short sitting, the finger in tashahhud, qunut in Subuh, dhikr aloud or quietly after the prayer, and the imam's du'a with the people saying amin.
4. Each step has **Bookmark**, **Note** and **Copy**; **Continue reading** and your bookmarks are at the top of the list. Steps with a doa have **Open in Doa & zikir ›** (e.g. after the prayer → Hisn al-Muslim's "what to say after completing the prayer"). The four imams, the commentators and the hadith graders show their death year (w. = wafat).
5. Sources for the schools: al-Nawawi (*al-Majmu'*, *Minhaj at-Talibin*), Ibn Qudamah (*al-Mughni*), Ibn Rushd (*Bidayat al-Mujtahid*); other scholars: al-Nawawi (*al-Majmu'*), al-Albani (*Asl Sifat Salat an-Nabi*), Ibn Baz (*Majmu' Fatawa*, *Fatawa Nur 'ala ad-Darb*), Ibn 'Uthaymin (*Majmu' Fatawa wa Rasa'il*, *al-Sharh al-Mumti'*), Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut (*Takhrij al-Musnad*, *Takhrij Zad al-Ma'ad*), Ibn Taymiyyah (*al-Fatawa al-Kubra*), Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah (numbered fatwas), each line with volume/page. Written for this app; for your own practice, follow your teacher.

**Solat Jumaat**

1. Home → **Solat Jumaat**: 7 steps, the same layout as Sifat Solat: preparing (ghusl, siwak, perfume), going early and what to do while waiting, **a qabliyyah sunnah before Jumu'ah?**, the khutbah (listening, tahiyyat al-masjid, the **pillars of the khutbah** in the four schools), **the sitting between the two khutbahs**, the prayer, and after it.
2. **Qabliyyah**: in the Prophet's time there was one adhan, when the imam sat on the minbar, and the khutbah followed (al-Bukhari 912); no authentic report shows him or the Companions praying after the Friday adhan. The four schools' views (Shafi'i and Hanafi: yes; Maliki: no fixed sunnah; Hanbali: two reports) and other scholars (Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Hajar, Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyyah, Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan) are given with references; the hadith "four before Jumu'ah" (Ibn Majah 1129) is very weak.
3. **MUIS Friday khutbah**: the latest khutbah from MUIS's website (Malay or English to match your language, with the PDF), checked every Thursday evening and Friday morning, and a link to all MUIS khutbahs.

**Kamus Al-Quran**

1. Home → **Kamus Al-Quran**: every word of the Qur'an by its root (1,650 roots and particles, 4,770 words). Tap a letter, or search in Arabic (root, word or a form as it appears) or by meaning in Malay/Indonesian or English (e.g. "rahmat").
2. A root shows each word from it (lemma) with its part of speech, how many times it occurs, its meanings (Indonesian, close to Malay, and English) and its forms; **Show the ayat** lists where it occurs: tap one to open the ayah (the **‹ Back** button returns to the Kamus).
3. Sources: the Quranic Arabic Corpus (University of Leeds, GNU GPL) with corrections from mustafa0x/quran-morphology; the meanings are Quran.com's word by word. The PTS *Kamus Al-Qur'an* is copyrighted, so it is not copied.
4. **Makna (meaning) anywhere**: long-press a word and choose **Makna** in the menu (or **Meaning** that appears on Sirah, Hadith and Doa pages). Arabic opens the Kamus; Malay or English offers a search in the Qur'an translations and links to Kamus Dewan (PRPM, DBP), Wiktionary, Cambridge and Google Translate. A fully open Malay dictionary that can be put inside the app does not exist yet.

**Sirah**

1. Home → **Sirah**: the life of the Prophet ﷺ as a timeline in three parts: **Before prophethood**, **Makkah** and **Madinah**. The language follows Settings → Translation language.
2. Tap an event for a short summary, then:
   - **Qur'an**: the ayat linked to the event by the hadith books or the tafsir. Tap to open them in the mushaf.
   - **Hadith**: the narrations in this app's books, with their gradings (Bukhari and Muslim are marked Sahih). Tap to read the hadith; **Open in …** takes you to it in the book.
   - **Reports in other books**: narrations from books not in this app; the heading names the books (e.g. Musnad Ahmad), and each has its **Source** (volume/page or number, grading) and **Open source ›**.
   - **Lineage**: under Birth of the Prophet, the nasab up to 'Adnan as al-Bukhari gives it, with the Arabic and the sources.
   - **The Prophet's family ﷺ**: at the top of the Sirah list: his wives (the Mothers of the Believers) in order, Mariyah and Rayhanah, his children, and his grandchildren through Zaynab, Ruqayyah and Fatimah, from Ibn al-Qayyim's Zad al-Ma'ad and Ibn Kathir's al-Bidayah wa an-Nihayah, with disagreements stated.
   - **Popular stories: check their status**: stories often told about the event, each with a coloured label: **No authentic basis** (red), **Weak** (orange), **Scholars differ** (amber), or **Historians' report: no checked chain** (grey: told by Ibn Ishaq and other historians, not authenticated in the hadith books). Each story names its **Source** (e.g. Ibn Hisham's *as-Sirah an-Nabawiyyah*, al-Bayhaqi's *Dala'il an-Nubuwwah*, with volume/page where checked). **Scholars cited, with their death dates** is at the bottom of the Sirah page.
3. An orange **!** in the timeline means the event has marked popular stories.
4. **‹ ›** at the bottom move to the previous or next event.
5. The three parts open and close when you tap them. **Search** (it stays at the top) finds events in Malay or English, e.g. "Badar", "Hijrah", "Ramadan". **Continue reading** at the top opens the last event you read.
6. On an event: **Bookmark** (listed under Bookmarked in the Sirah and in the Bookmarks button's **Sirah** tab), **Note** (also in Notes, under **Sirah**) and **Copy** (the summary, references and story statuses).
7. Tapping an ayah or **Open in …** on a hadith takes you there; the phone's **back** button (or the **‹ Back** button over the mushaf) returns to the same event.
8. **Meaning of a word:** long-press a word in the Sirah (also in Hadith and Doa pages) to select it, then tap **Meaning** (or **Makna** in the phone's selection menu, anywhere in the app): an Arabic word opens the **Kamus Al-Quran** (or Almaany if it is not a Qur'an word); a Malay or English word offers a search in the Qur'an translations and Kamus Dewan (DBP), Wiktionary, Cambridge or Google Translate.
9. The summaries are written for this app from the hadith and the named historians; dates the hadith do not fix (e.g. 12 Rabi' al-Awwal, 27 Rajab) are marked as such.

**Scan & check**

1. Someone posts an "ayah" or a "hadith" and you want to check it. Home → **Scan & check**, then:
   - **Camera** (Android app): choose the language of the text first (**Arabic** or **Melayu / English**), take a photo of the text, and the app reads it on the phone (no internet needed for reading). Arabic is read by a reader made for Arabic script (from EasyOCR), which handles harakat and Qur'an script; the first time you scan Arabic, the app asks to download it once (about 59 MB, Wi-Fi recommended); Malay and English by Google ML Kit.
   - **Read with Google Lens**: if a reading is not right, Lens (in the Google app) reads the last picture, or opens its own camera. Select the text, tap **Share → My Personal Quran**, and it is checked here. Lens needs the internet.
   - **Picture / screenshot**: pick a screenshot or photo you already have.
   - **Share**: in WhatsApp, Telegram, the browser or the gallery, tap **Share → My Personal Quran** ("Check in My Quran"). Shared text is checked at once; for a shared picture, choose its language.
   - **Paste**: paste the text into the box. This works in the browser too.
   - The language of this page, the Sirah and Masjid & musollah follows Settings → **Language of the Sirah, Scan & check and Masjid pages** (Same as translation, Bahasa Melayu or English).
2. Check the text in the box (the camera can misread a letter, especially in Arabic with harakat or decorated fonts), correct it if needed, and tap **Check**.
3. **Qur'an**: green **Found in the Qur'an**, **Found: part of an ayah**, amber **Close to an ayah: there are differences**, or red **No ayah matched**. Each match shows the ayah, its translation and how much of it matched; tap it to open the ayah (the phone's **back** button returns to your check).
4. **Check outside the app** (shown after every check): **Semak Hadis**, **Dorar.net** and **Sunnah.com**; your text is copied first (paste it in Semak Hadis's search box).
5. **Hadith**: the eight books in this app are searched (the first time, every kitab is downloaded, so use Wi-Fi). Each match shows the book, number and gradings, and whether the whole text is in that hadith or **only part of it**. A red **Not found** is not proof that a hadith is fabricated: it may be in other books (such as Musnad Ahmad) or in other words. Ask a qualified teacher.
5. How it matches: Arabic, the same letters and words (harakat, alif and hamza ignored, so the mushaf's Uthmani spelling and plain spelling compare the same, and a few misread words do not stop a match); Malay and English, the same important words. Translators word things differently, so a low percentage does not mean it is absent. Malay hadith text is compared with the Indonesian translation.

**Halal food**

1. Home → **Halal food**. Type a name or area (3 letters or more), e.g. *Tampines* or *prata*: the app searches **MUIS's halal directory** directly and lists each place with its type, address and certificate number, and **Map ›**. MUIS shows at most 200 results; narrow the search. (Android app; needs the internet.)
2. Certificates can expire or be withdrawn: check the MUIS certificate displayed at the premises.
3. **Official links** for Japan (JNTO's guide for Muslim travelers and its Muslim Welcome Guide, plus Halal Gourmet Japan, a private directory JNTO points to), Korea (Korea Tourism Organization) and Thailand (CICOT, Halal Standard Institute) open in the browser. Korea's list has four grades and only **Halal Certified** is certified halal; Japan has no government halal body.

**Masjid & musollah**

1. Home → **Masjid & musollah**: the 70 mosques in MUIS's Mosque Directory (with address and phone), 3 mosques not under MUIS, and the few musollahs in OpenStreetMap and Wikipedia.
2. Tap the **location** button to list the nearest first. **Directions** opens Google Maps; **Call** phones the mosque.
3. **Search** by name, street or postal code. **All · Masjid · Musollah · My places** filter the list.
4. **Add a place**: name, type, address and notes (level, women's area, wudhu), and its location: **My location now** (stand at the place) or paste a Google Maps link. Your places are kept on the phone; tap **Edit** on one to change or delete it.
5. Opening hours, facilities and Friday prayer arrangements are not listed; check with the mosque or MUIS.

**Notes**

1. Home → **Notes**: all your notes, newest first. Filter by **Qur'an**, **Hadith** or **Free notes**, or search.
2. **New note** writes a free note. For an ayah, long-press it → **Write a note**; for a hadith, tap its note button. Notes save as you type.
3. Tap a note to edit it; **Delete** removes it (after you confirm); **Open the ayah / Open the hadith** takes you there.
4. Notes are kept on the phone only. **Copy all notes** copies them all, so you can keep a copy elsewhere.

**Prayer times**

- **‹ ›** move by day; the next prayer is in gold.
- The Hijri date under the date opens the **Hijri calendar**: each month with the Gregorian and Hijri day in every box, Islamic days marked (Awal Muharram, Asyura, Maulidur Rasul, Israk Mikraj, Nisfu Syaaban, 1 Ramadan, Nuzul al-Quran, the last ten nights, Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Arafah, Hari Raya Aidiladha, the days of Tasyrik) and listed under **This month**. The dates are calculated for Singapore with the MABIMS criteria that MUIS uses. If MUIS announces a different date (the moon is sighted or not), use **Adjust the Hijri date** (−2 to +2 days); it changes the Hijri date everywhere in the app.
- **Location**: **Singapore (MUIS)** official timetable, or **My location / Choose a city**. Cities are grouped by country (Southeast and East Asia including Japan, Korea, China, Hong Kong and Taiwan; South Asia; the Middle East; Europe; North America; Australia and New Zealand). Each city uses its own country's method (e.g. JAKIM for Malaysia, Kemenag for Indonesia, Umm al-Qura for Saudi Arabia, Diyanet for Turkey; others the Muslim World League), named under the times, and the times are in that city's own clock.
- **Adjust (minutes)** for each prayer.

**Alerts** (Android)

1. Tap the **bell** beside each prayer to turn its alert on or off.
2. **Sound**: Makkah adhan, Madinah adhan, Short tone, **Chime**, **Bell**, **My short tone** (any audio file of yours, played only for its first 6–20 seconds, about the length of the opening "Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar"), **Notification only** (a notification with no sound at all, for the reminder and Syuruk too), or **None** (no adhan alert at all; the reminder before the prayer and the notification bar still work if they are on). For an adhan, tap **Choose … adhan file** and pick a recording on your phone.
3. **Reminder before each prayer**: Off to 30 minutes.
4. **Test the sound** / **Stop**. If alerts do not come, allow notifications and alarms, and remove battery limits.
5. Alerts are **on by default** for the five prayers, and the app asks once to allow notifications. They follow the phone's sound mode: **ring** = the adhan or tone plays; **vibrate** = the phone vibrates with the notification, no sound; **silent or Do Not Disturb** = a silent notification only. **Test the sound** follows the same rule (on vibrate it only vibrates). With earphones or a Bluetooth earpiece connected, the adhan plays in the earphones only.
6. **Prayer times in the notification bar**: **Always** (default) keeps today's times and the time left to the next prayer in the pull-down bar; **30 min before each prayer** shows it only in the half hour before each prayer; **Off**. Tap it to open Prayer times.

**Qibla**

1. Home → **Qibla**; allow location.
2. Hold the phone flat and turn until it says **Facing the qibla**.
3. If asked, move the phone in a figure 8 to calibrate. Keep away from metal and magnetic cases.

**Backup and restore**

1. Settings → **Back up or restore settings, bookmarks and notes** (also linked from Notes).
2. **Save a backup file** saves one .json file with all your settings, Qur'an and hadith bookmarks, My ruku' and My blocks, notes, Hafazan results and last-read places. Choose where it goes, e.g. Google Drive, or send it to yourself.
3. **Restore from a backup file** on this or a new phone replaces what is in the app with the backup, then reopens the app.
4. Not included: Hafazan voice recordings and the adhan files you picked (choose those again), and downloaded texts (they download again).

**Sync between your phone and tablet**

1. Settings → Back up or restore → **Sync with your other devices (Google)…**.
2. Tap **Sign in with Google** on **every device**, with the **same Google account**. The data is kept in a hidden folder of this app in your own Google Drive (the app cannot see your other Drive files). The first time, Google warns that the app is unverified (it is your own, in testing): tap **Continue**. Google may ask you to sign in again about once a week. (A device without Google services can still use the older way under *Other way*: one file in Drive or OneDrive, created on the first device and chosen on the others.)
3. After that, bookmarks (Qur'an, hadith, Sirah, Sifat Solat, Jumaat, Doa), notes, last-read places, My ruku' and My blocks, Hafazan results and your own places sync on their own: when the app opens, when you come back to it, and a few seconds after a change. A deletion on one device is deleted on the other. **Sync now** forces it.
4. It is two-way: whichever device you change, the others get it at their next sync. The Sync window shows **which device last wrote, and when**. A red line means the last attempt failed (usually no internet). Settings, prayer alerts and voice recordings are not synced.

## 10. Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "Printed pages need internet" | Connect once, or Save all 604 pages |
| Recitation does not play | Check the internet; the message in brackets gives the cause |
| Red words ahead of or behind the voice | Listen box → Red words follow the voice |
| Hafazan hears too little | Microphone: quiet voice → High or Maximum |
| Many words marked skipped | Recite a little slower and clearly |
| Speech recognition not available | Install or update the Google app, or use Type |
| Prayer alert did not sound | Allow notifications and alarms; remove battery limits; Test the sound |
| Qibla arrow jumps | Calibrate (figure 8); remove magnetic case |
| A box will not close | Tap X, tap outside it, or press Back |
